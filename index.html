3. Recipes

Antipasto and Caprese

Chef: Ana

  1. Thursday around 4:00: arrange the charcuterie meats, cheddar (sliced or cubed), olives, and artichoke hearts on boards or platters, and keep them covered in the fridge.
  2. Caprese: alternate slices of fresh mozzarella and sliced tomato on a platter, tuck in the basil leaves, then drizzle with olive oil and sprinkle with salt and pepper. Assemble it close to serving (around 4:30) so the tomatoes don't get watery.
  3. Put the platters out as guests arrive (about 4:30-5:00). Keep the cheese and meats out of the fridge for no more than 2 hours.

Put a small bowl of toothpicks and a small plate or napkin next to the olives and artichokes. Keep a spoon in the caprese for guests to serve themselves.

Minestrone Milan Style (triple batch, makes about 18 small bowls)

Chef: Michael

From The Talisman Italian Cook Book, with the changes: no salt pork, no sage, no pea beans, and no peas. Pasta is cooked separately. Make it Wednesday.

IngredientAmount
Olive oil1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Garlic, chopped2-3 cloves
Medium onions, chopped3
Fresh parsley, chopped1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Celery, chopped9 stalks
Carrots, sliced6
Potatoes, diced6
Small cabbage, shredded3/4 head
Zucchini, diced6
Tomato paste (diluted in 3 cups warm water)3 tablespoons
Stock or water4 1/2 quarts
Elbow macaroni (cooked separately)3 cups, uncooked
Grated Romano (served on the side)3/4 cup
Salt and pepperTo taste
  1. Heat the oil in a large stockpot. Add the garlic, onions, and parsley and cook until soft, without browning too far.
  2. Add the diluted tomato paste, salt, and pepper. Cook 5 minutes.
  3. Add the celery, carrots, potatoes, cabbage, and zucchini, then the stock or water.
  4. Simmer about 45-60 minutes, until all the vegetables are tender.
  5. Remove 3-4 ladles of vegetables and broth, puree in the food processor, and stir back in. This makes it richer and less watery.
  6. Adjust the thickness with extra stock if needed. Taste and season.
  7. Cool fast: divide into shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours.

Thursday: reheat on the stove, stirring, to a gentle simmer (165°F). Cook the macaroni separately until al dente, drain, and toss with a little olive oil. Serve the pasta and the Romano on the side. Don't store the pasta in the soup.

Mashed Potatoes - make Wednesday

Chef: Carmine

IngredientAmount
Yukon Gold potatoes8-9 lb
Salted butter2 cups (4 sticks), plus extra for dotting
Whole milk or cream2-3 cups
SaltIn the boiling water; add more at the end only after tasting (the salted butter adds some)
  1. Peel and cut the potatoes into 2-inch chunks. Cover with cold salted water.
  2. Boil 15-20 minutes until fork-tender. Drain well and return to the warm pot for 1-2 minutes to dry out.
  3. Warm the butter and milk together. Mash and add the warm mixture gradually. Keep them slightly creamier than normal, since they firm up overnight.
  4. Spread into 2 buttered dishes (or containers that fit your slow cookers), dot with butter, cover, and refrigerate.

Thursday (no oven needed): put the cold potatoes in one or two slow cookers on HIGH with a splash of warm milk and butter. Stir every 30 minutes. They take about 2-2.5 hours to reach 165°F. Then switch to WARM (140°F or above). Or use a big covered pot on low heat and stir often.

Stuffing - Betty Crocker Bread Stuffing + golden raisins + Granny Smith apples

Chef: Anthony

Based on the Betty Crocker Bread Stuffing recipe (one batch = 9 cups bread = one 9x13 pan). Tripled for 18 people (3 pans), with raisins and apples added. Original recipe. The recipe has no stock; the butter gives the moisture.

IngredientTripled amount
Butter2 1/4 cups (4 1/2 sticks)
Celery, chopped6 large stalks
Medium onions, chopped3 (about 1 1/2 cups)
Country white bread, toasted and cubed3 loaves (1 loaf per batch, about 27 cups of cubes)
Fresh thyme leaves, chopped (or dried)1 1/2 tablespoons (or 1 1/2 tsp dried)
Salt (reduced, since the butter is salted)1 teaspoon to start for the full tripled batch (the original recipe uses 1 tsp per batch). Taste the mixture and add more if needed.
Ground sage1 1/2 teaspoons
Pepper3/4 teaspoon
Add-in: golden raisinsabout 1 1/2 cups
Add-in: Granny Smith apples, sliced or diced3
  1. Wednesday: toast the country white bread, cut it into cubes, and store the cubes in bags. Chop the celery and onion.
  2. Thursday morning: melt the butter in a large pot (a 6-8 qt pot, or do it in two batches). Cook the celery and onion 6-8 minutes until tender. Take it off the heat.
  3. In a very large bowl or clean roasting pan, gently toss the celery mixture with the bread cubes, thyme, salt, sage, pepper, raisins, and apples until evenly coated.
  4. Divide into 3 greased 9x13 pans. Cover and refrigerate until baking.
  5. Bake two pans in the top oven (side by side on one rack) and one pan in the bottom oven beside the ham, at 325°F, covered with foil, for 30 minutes. Then uncover and bake 15 minutes more. If the pans go in cold from the fridge, add about 10 minutes.

Anthony tosses the stuffing and slices the apples with supervision. An adult does the onion and celery chopping and the stove.

Turkey Breast + Gravy

Chef: Michael

Brine (Wednesday evening to Thursday morning)

For two 6-7 lb bone-in breasts, split lengthwise into 4 halves (about 3-3.5 lb each) so they lie flat and cook in the top oven. The turkey must be fully thawed first.

IngredientAmount
Water2 gallons (1 qt hot, the rest cold)
Diamond Crystal kosher salt1 1/2 cups (or 3/4 cup Morton kosher; not table salt)
Brown sugar (optional)1/2 cup
Black peppercorns2 tablespoons
Orange zest, in strips3 oranges
Fresh thyme / sage / rosemary8 sprigs / 10-12 leaves / 3-4 sprigs
Bay leaves3-4
Garlic, smashed (optional)6 cloves
  1. Heat 1 qt of the water with the salt, sugar, peppercorns, zest, herbs, bay, and garlic. Stir to dissolve and simmer 5 minutes.
  2. Add the remaining cold water (plus ice if needed). The brine must be under 40°F before the turkey goes in.
  3. Put the breasts in a brining bag, bucket, or cooler and cover with brine. Keep at 40°F or below (use ice packs in a cooler if the fridge is full).
  4. Brine 12-16 hours (the smaller halves take up salt faster), never more than 24. Discard the brine, rinse briefly, and pat very dry.
  5. Set on a rack, uncovered in the fridge, until roasting, to dry the skin.

Roast

  1. Rub the turkey halves with softened unsalted butter (about 1/2 stick per half) and chopped herbs (thyme, sage, parsley), including under the skin. No added salt, since the brine already salted the meat. Leave out of the fridge 30-45 minutes before roasting.
  2. Roast skin side up in the top oven at 325°F, on a rack in a half-sheet pan (18x13 in) or a roasting pan up to about 16x13 in, with a little stock in the bottom. Use the lower rack position, with at least 2 in of space above the meat. Start checking at 1 hour. Each half is done at 165°F in the thickest part, usually 1-1 1/2 hours. Tent loosely with foil if the skin browns too fast.
  3. Move the turkey halves to a clean pan or cutting board and pour the drippings into a pot for the gravy. Rest tented with foil for 30-45 minutes before carving. The halves hold above 140°F for about an hour. For a longer hold, wrap in foil and towels or set in a cooler. Check the temperature before carving and warm the slices with hot gravy if needed.
  4. Gravy (about 6 cups): pour the drippings into a pot and add butter to make about 1/2 cup of fat. Whisk in 1/2 cup flour and cook 2 minutes. Slowly whisk in 6 cups warm stock. Simmer 10 minutes. Use low-sodium or unsalted stock, and taste before adding any salt, since brined drippings and salted butter are salty. Two breasts don't give many drippings, so have extra stock ready.

Ham with Brown Sugar Glaze

Chef: Leo

  1. Use a fully cooked 8-10 lb ham. Take it out of the fridge 1 hour before.
  2. Glaze: stir together 1 cup brown sugar, 1/4 cup Dijon or honey, and 2-3 tablespoons water or apple juice.
  3. Bake at 325°F, covered in foil, about 10 minutes per lb until it reaches 140°F. In the last 20 minutes, uncover, brush on the glaze, and raise the oven to 400°F.
  4. Rest, tented, 15 minutes before slicing. An adult handles the oven and carving.

Green Beans

Chef: Dominic

Ingredients: 4 lb green beans, olive oil, butter, salt. Optional: lemon.

  1. Wednesday: trim the beans.
  2. Thursday: boil in well-salted water for 4-5 minutes (in two batches if needed) until crisp-tender. Drain.
  3. Return to the hot pot and toss with olive oil and a few tablespoons of butter. Taste before adding salt, since the butter is salted. A squeeze of lemon is optional.

Plain green beans only, with no peas or mixed vegetable blends. To get ahead, blanch and ice-shock them in advance, then reheat in a skillet with the oil and butter at 5:00.

Citrus Salad

Chef: Michael

IngredientAmount
Spring mix + arugula, or romaine heartsabout 2 lb mix (two 1 lb tubs), or 4 large romaine hearts
Cucumbers, sliced3-4
Carrots, peeled into ribbons or shredded4

Dressing (about 1 1/4 cups, enough for 2 lb of greens): 3/4 cup olive oil, 1/2 cup lemon juice (about 3 lemons), 1 1/2 teaspoons salt, 1-2 teaspoons sugar, and pepper. Shake in a jar, taste, and adjust the sugar and lemon.

  1. Wednesday: make the dressing and refrigerate it.
  2. Thursday afternoon: wash and dry the greens, slice the cucumbers, and prepare the carrots. Keep them in the fridge in separate containers.
  3. Toss with the dressing right before serving (around 5:15) so the greens stay crisp.

Cranberry Sauce, Cornbread (store-bought), Chestnuts

Chef: Dominic (cranberry sauce and chestnuts); Michael (cornbread)

  • Cranberry: chill 3 cans, then open, slice or spoon into a bowl.
  • Cornbread: store-bought. Put it covered in foil in the bottom oven at 325°F for about 10-15 minutes to warm, once the ham comes out.
  • Roasted chestnuts: cut an X in each flat side and bake on a sheet at 400°F for 20-25 minutes until the shells curl. Wrap in a towel for 5 minutes, then peel while warm. Do this after dinner when the oven is free.

5. Plan

Uses your double oven: the top oven (10 in tall) roasts the split turkey breasts early, then takes two of the three stuffing pans at 4:30 (two 9x13 pans fit side by side on one rack). The bottom oven (15 in tall) takes the ham and the third stuffing pan. The potatoes and soup stay off the ovens.

Monday and Tuesday

  • Finish all shopping and read every ingredient list (nut ingredients, peas, lentils, pea protein; "may contain nuts" is OK).
  • Check that the turkey is thawing in the fridge. It must be fully thawed by Wednesday evening, since it has to be thawed before brining. Breasts need 2-3 days, a whole bird 4-5. If it's still frozen, use the cold-water method.
  • Confirm with Ana and Halmoni what they bring.
  • Check that you have 1-2 slow cookers and enough roasting pans, foil, and a meat thermometer.
  • Clear fridge space for the soup and potatoes.
