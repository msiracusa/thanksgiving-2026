Minestrone Milan Style (triple batch, makes about 18 small bowls)

Chef: Michael

From The Talisman Italian Cook Book, with the changes: no salt pork, no sage, no pea beans, and no peas. Pasta is cooked separately. Make it Thursday afternoon.

IngredientAmount
Olive oil1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Garlic, chopped2-3 cloves
Medium onions, chopped3
Fresh parsley, chopped1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Celery, chopped9 stalks
Baby carrots, sliced (no peeling needed)about 1 lb (about 3 cups sliced)
Potatoes, diced6
Small cabbage, shredded3/4 head
Zucchini, diced6
Tomato paste (diluted in 3 cups warm water)3 tablespoons
Stock or water4 1/2 quarts
Elbow macaroni (cooked separately)3 cups, uncooked
Grated Romano (served on the side)3/4 cup
Salt and pepperTo taste
  1. Heat the oil in a large stockpot. Add the garlic, onions, and parsley and cook until soft, without browning too far.
  2. Add the diluted tomato paste, salt, and pepper. Cook 5 minutes.
  3. Add the celery, carrots, potatoes, cabbage, and zucchini, then the stock or water.
  4. Simmer about 45-60 minutes, until all the vegetables are tender.
  5. Remove 3-4 ladles of vegetables and broth, puree in the food processor, and stir back in. This makes it richer and less watery.
  6. Adjust the thickness with extra stock if needed. Taste and season.
  7. Turn off the heat, cover, and leave it on the stove until you reheat it at about 4:30 PM (about 1 1/2 hours).

To serve: at about 4:30 PM bring the soup back to a boil, then simmer gently (165°F) until dinner, stirring occasionally. Cook the macaroni separately around 4:00 PM until al dente, drain, and toss with a little olive oil; leave it covered at room temperature. Serve the pasta and the Pecorino Romano on the side. Don't store the pasta in the soup.

Mashed Potatoes - make Thursday afternoon

Chef: Carmine

IngredientAmount
Yukon Gold potatoes8-9 lb
Salted butter2 cups (4 sticks), plus extra for dotting
Whole milk or cream2-3 cups
SaltIn the boiling water; add more at the end only after tasting (the salted butter adds some)
  1. About 11:30 AM: peel and cut the potatoes into 2-inch chunks. Cover with cold salted water until you boil them.
  2. About 12:50 PM: bring to a boil and cook 15-20 minutes until fork-tender. Drain well and return to the warm pot for 1-2 minutes to dry out.
  3. Warm the butter and milk together (the microwave works). Mash and add the warm mixture gradually. Season to taste.
  4. Spread into 2 buttered oven-safe dishes (the 9x13x1.5 in and an 8x8x2.5 in Pyrex), dot with butter, and cover tightly with foil.
  5. Hold hot, off the stove: put the dishes in the top oven at 200°F (about 1:30 PM). At 3:20 PM take them out and wrap them in towels in a cooler, where they stay above 140°F for about 2 hours. Just before serving, stir in a splash of warm milk to restore the creaminess. Check that they are still above 140°F.

Stuffing - Betty Crocker Bread Stuffing + golden raisins + Granny Smith apples

Chef: Anthony

Based on the Betty Crocker Bread Stuffing recipe (one batch = 9 cups bread = one 9x13 pan). Tripled for 18 people (3 pans), with raisins and apples added. Original recipe. The recipe has no stock; the butter gives the moisture.

IngredientTripled amount
Butter2 1/4 cups (4 1/2 sticks)
Celery, chopped6 large stalks
Medium onions, chopped3 (about 1 1/2 cups)
Country white bread, toasted and cubed3 loaves (1 loaf per batch, about 27 cups of cubes)
Fresh thyme leaves, chopped (or dried)1 1/2 tablespoons (or 1 1/2 tsp dried)
Salt (reduced, since the butter is salted)1 teaspoon to start for the full tripled batch (the original recipe uses 1 tsp per batch). Taste the mixture and add more if needed.
Fresh sage, finely chopped2 tablespoons (add more to taste)
Pepper3/4 teaspoon
Add-in: golden raisinsabout 1 1/2 cups
Add-in: Granny Smith apples, sliced or diced3
  1. Wednesday night: toast the country white bread in your large toaster (6 slices per round, about 6 minutes with unloading), cutting each batch into cubes while the next one toasts. Store the cubes in bags on the counter. 3 loaves (about 45 slices) make the 27 cups; the 4th loaf is a spare.
  2. Thursday: chop the celery and onion (about 10:30 AM) and keep them covered on the counter.
  3. Thursday at 2:30 PM: melt the butter in a large deep saucepan (about 4-5 qt, or do it in two batches). Cook the celery and onion 6-8 minutes until tender. Take it off the heat.
  4. In a very large bowl or clean roasting pan, gently toss the celery mixture with the bread cubes, thyme, salt, sage, pepper, raisins, and apples until evenly coated.
  5. Divide into 3 greased pans (about 12 cups in the 9x13x3 in, 10 cups in the 9x13x2.5 in Pyrex, and 5 cups in an 8x8x2.5 in Pyrex). Cover with foil and leave on the counter until baking at 4:00 PM (about 1 1/2 hours). A squeeze of lemon juice on the apples keeps them from browning.

If the pans go in cold from the fridge, add about 10 minutes.

Ham with Brown Sugar Glaze

Chef: Leo

Three fully cooked Boar's Head Sweet Slice hams (about 3.25 lb each; one is apple wood smoked). Cooking instructions from the package: bake at 325°F for about 15 minutes per pound, covered tightly with foil, then uncover and bake at 425°F for the final 15 minutes with extra glaze.

  1. Take the hams out of the fridge about 30-45 minutes before heating.
  2. Glaze (make Thursday morning): stir together 1 cup brown sugar, 1/4 cup Dijon or honey, and 2-3 tablespoons water or apple juice.
  3. Score the hams: cut a shallow diamond pattern across the top of each ham, about 1/4 inch deep and 1 inch apart, so the glaze can drip down into the ham.
  4. Put the scored hams in a roasting pan (all three fit in one 16x13 pan, or use two 9x13 pans) with about 1/8 inch of water in the bottom. Cover tightly with foil and bake in the top oven at 325°F for about 15 minutes per pound (about 50 minutes for 3.25 lb hams).
  5. Uncover, brush on extra glaze, raise the oven to 425°F, and bake the final 15 minutes, until the center reaches 140°F. In all, plan about 65 minutes.
  6. Rest, tented, 10-15 minutes before slicing. An adult handles the oven and carving.

Turkey Breast + Gravy

Chef: Michael

Dry brine (Wednesday evening, about 6:00 PM; 12-24 hours before roasting)

Based on J. Kenji López-Alt's dry brine in the Serious Eats article How to Brine a Turkey for Thanksgiving. The article's starting batch is 6 tbsp Morton kosher salt (or 1/2 cup Diamond Crystal) with 2 tbsp baking powder. It says you will most likely not need all of it, and sometimes less than half is enough. The turkey should be well coated, but not encrusted. Brine uncovered on a rack in a rimmed pan for 12 to 24 hours, then roast without rinsing and without any more salt. (If you go longer than 24 hours, up to 3 days, cover it loosely with plastic wrap or cheesecloth.) The baking powder helps the skin brown and crisp. You are brining and roasting the two fresh breasts whole (no splitting). The article says aromatics add little flavor in a brine, so there are none in the brine. For flavor, put herb sprigs and orange zest in the pan under the rack.

IngredientAmount for 2 breasts
Morton kosher saltMix 3 tablespoons (half of the Serious Eats starting batch of 6 tbsp). You will likely use about 2 to 2 1/2 tbsp of it; stop when the skin is evenly coated but not encrusted. Morton is about 60% of the volume of Diamond Crystal. The turkeys have nothing added (not basted or enhanced), so no reduction is needed.
Baking powder, aluminum-free (not baking soda)Mix 1 tablespoon (half of the 2 tbsp starting batch), keeping the 3-to-1 salt-to-baking-powder ratio. You will use only as much as goes with the salt.
  1. Pat both breasts completely dry with paper towels, including the skin and the inside rib cavity. Whisk the salt and baking powder together in one bowl. You will probably not use all of it. Apply it until the turkey is well coated but not encrusted, and discard what is left over.
  2. Sprinkle the mixture on all surfaces of each breast: the skin and top, the sides, the underside, and the rib cavity. Hold your hand 6-10 inches above the turkey and let the mixture shower down for even coverage. Don't pile it up in one spot. There is nothing under the skin.
  3. Set both breasts skin side up on the wire rack in your roasting pan, with 1-2 inches of space between them. Leave it uncovered in the fridge for 12 to 24 hours (about 19-20 hours if you start at 6:00 PM Wednesday for a 2:15 PM roast). Use this same pan and rack for roasting. Loose salt and some salty liquid will collect in the pan overnight, so you empty and rinse the pan before roasting (see the roast steps).
  4. Thursday: do not rinse and do not add more salt.

Roast (Thursday, bottom oven)

  1. About 45 minutes before roasting, take the turkey out of the fridge. Blot any wet spots with paper towels. Preheat the bottom oven to 425°F (400°F if the oven is convection).
  2. Lift the turkey (still on its rack) onto a clean tray. Do not rinse the turkey. Pour out the liquid and the loose salt, then rinse and dry the pan and the rack, put the rack and turkey back, and pour about 1 cup of unsalted stock or water into the pan under the rack, so the drippings don't scorch. Add a few sprigs of thyme, sage, and rosemary and a few strips of orange zest to the liquid for flavor. Brush or rub each breast lightly with 1 to 1 1/2 tablespoons of vegetable oil (not butter, which can burn at 425°F). Keep the breasts on the rack, spaced apart so heat circulates.
  3. Roast for about 75-100 minutes (the original recipe says 65-85 for one breast, but two breasts in one oven take a bit longer), rotating the pan halfway through for even browning. Start checking at 60 minutes. Pull each breast when the thickest part (away from the bone) reaches 158°F. One may finish 5-10 minutes before the other, so pull the first one first.
  4. Move the breasts to a clean pan or cutting board and rest, tented loosely with foil, for 20-30 minutes. The temperature will rise during the rest. Check that it reaches 165°F in the thickest part before carving; if it does not, return it to the oven for a few minutes. A whole breast holds above 140°F for about an hour. For a longer hold, wrap in foil and towels or set it in a cooler.
  5. Gravy (about 6 cups): (drippings from a dry-brined turkey are salty: taste them first, use less of them and more unsalted stock if strong, and add no salt until you taste the finished gravy.) pour the pan drippings (and the stock or water under the rack) into a fat separator or measuring cup. Skim off the fat, and measure 6-8 tbsp (top up with unsalted butter if needed). Melt it in a saucepan, whisk in 6-8 tbsp all-purpose flour, and cook 2 minutes. Slowly whisk in the drippings and about 5 cups of warm stock. Simmer 8-10 minutes until it coats a spoon. Use low-sodium or unsalted stock, and taste before adding any salt, since the drippings are salty. Pepper to taste.

Green Beans

Chef: Dominic

Ingredients: 4 lb green beans, olive oil, butter, salt. Optional: lemon.

  1. Trim the beans (any time Thursday). At about 4:35 PM boil them in well-salted water for 4-5 minutes in one large pot until crisp-tender (two batches if the pot is small). Drain.

5. Plan

Uses your double oven and 5 burners (3 small in back, 2 large in front). Use the two front burners first, and the back burners only if both front ones are busy. This plan never needs more than the two front burners at once. Only the stuffing bread is done Wednesday night; everything else is cooked Thursday. The bottom oven (15 in tall) bakes the cornbread muffins in the morning, roasts the two whole turkey breasts from 2:30 PM at 425°F, then takes all three stuffing pans once the turkey is out (two on one rack, one on a second rack). Burners: no more than 3 at once, because the pasta is cooked early and the gravy is reheated in the microwave.

4:00 PMOnce the turkey is out of the bottom oven, set it to 325°F (open the door for a few minutes to cool it faster). Put in all three stuffing pans (two on one rack, one on a second rack), covered. They need about 45-55 minutes in all (longer if cold). Cook the macaroni, drain, toss with oil, and leave it covered at room temperature.
4:15 PMAssemble the caprese and put out the antipasto. Check that the potatoes are still above 140°F.
4:25 PMUncover the hams, brush on extra glaze, raise the top oven to 425°F, and bake 15 more minutes (until 140°F).
4:30 PMBring the soup back to a boil, then simmer gently to 165°F.
4:35 PMCheck the turkey temperature (it should be above 140°F), then carve it and moisten with hot gravy. Boil the green beans (4-5 minutes), drain, and toss with olive oil and butter. Uncover the stuffing pans.
4:40 PMHams out (140°F), tent them to rest for 10-15 minutes. Put the cornbread muffins (covered in foil) in the bottom oven beside the stuffing (325°F) to warm for 5-10 minutes. Take the stuffing out when golden (about 4:50).
4:55 PMBuffer: 10 minutes to catch up on anything running late, taste and season, and check that everything is hot.
5:05 PMPut the cold dishes on the table (cranberry sauce, kimchi, bread, Pecorino, crackers).
5:10 PMPut the hot sides on the table: mashed potatoes, stuffing, green beans, cornbread muffins.
5:15 PMReheat the gravy in the microwave. Toss the salad. Stir warm milk into the potatoes. Slice the ham. Put out the pasta and the Pecorino Romano.
5:30 PMDinner. Serve the soup first, then the main course.
6:15 PMPreheat the bottom oven to 400°F.
6:30 PMClear the table and put leftovers away (within 2 hours of cooking). Chestnuts into the oven (400°F, 20-25 min).
6:55 PMChestnuts out; wrap in a towel for 5 minutes, then peel while warm.
7:00 PMDessert: apple pie, chestnuts, and any desserts guests brought.
Why this works: nothing cooked needs fridge space. The mashed potatoes hold hot in the top oven and then a towel-wrapped cooler, the soup stays covered on the stove (about 1 1/2 hours, within the 2-hour limit), and the stuffing waits 1 1/2 hours on the counter before baking. The pasta is cooked early and the gravy is reheated in the microwave, so you never need more than 2 burners at once, both on the front. The turkey must be out of the bottom oven by about 4:00 PM so the stuffing can go in. If it is running late, tent it and let it finish; the stuffing can wait a few minutes. The dry brine is short (about 5 hours), so salt the turkey Wednesday evening instead if you can spare a shelf.

Food safety

  • Refrigerate cooked food within 2 hours. Cool large batches in shallow containers.
  • Keep the fridge at 40°F or below.
  • Raw turkey: keep it away from other food, wash hands, and wash the board, knife, and sink with hot soapy water after handling it.
  • Reheat soup and potatoes to 165°F. Turkey to 165°F. Ham to 140°F.
  • Keep hot food at 140°F or above until served.
