Antipasto and Caprese

Chef: Ana

Minestrone Milan Style (triple batch, makes about 18 small bowls)

Chef: Michael

From The Talisman Italian Cook Book, with the changes: no salt pork, no sage, no pea beans, and no peas. Pasta is cooked separately. Make it Thursday afternoon.

IngredientAmount
Olive oil1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Garlic, chopped2-3 cloves
Medium onions, chopped3
Fresh parsley, chopped1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Celery, chopped9 stalks
Carrots, sliced6
Potatoes, diced6
Small cabbage, shredded3/4 head
Zucchini, diced6
Tomato paste (diluted in 3 cups warm water)3 tablespoons
Stock or water4 1/2 quarts
Elbow macaroni (cooked separately)3 cups, uncooked
Grated Romano (served on the side)3/4 cup
Salt and pepperTo taste
  1. Heat the oil in a large stockpot. Add the garlic, onions, and parsley and cook until soft, without browning too far.
  2. Add the diluted tomato paste, salt, and pepper. Cook 5 minutes.
  3. Add the celery, carrots, potatoes, cabbage, and zucchini, then the stock or water.
  4. Simmer about 45-60 minutes, until all the vegetables are tender.
  5. Remove 3-4 ladles of vegetables and broth, puree in the food processor, and stir back in. This makes it richer and less watery.
  6. Adjust the thickness with extra stock if needed. Taste and season.
  7. Turn off the heat, cover, and leave it on the stove until you reheat it at about 4:45 PM (about 1 1/2 hours).

To serve: at about 4:45 PM bring the soup back to a boil, then simmer gently (165°F) until dinner, stirring occasionally. Cook the macaroni separately around 4:15 PM until al dente, drain, and toss with a little olive oil; leave it covered at room temperature. Serve the pasta and the Pecorino Romano on the side. Don't store the pasta in the soup.

Mashed Potatoes - make Thursday afternoon

Chef: Carmine

IngredientAmount
Yukon Gold potatoes8-9 lb
Salted butter2 cups (4 sticks), plus extra for dotting
Whole milk or cream2-3 cups
SaltIn the boiling water; add more at the end only after tasting (the salted butter adds some)
  1. About 11:30 AM: peel and cut the potatoes into 2-inch chunks. Cover with cold salted water until you boil them.
  2. About 12:50 PM: bring to a boil and cook 15-20 minutes until fork-tender. Drain well and return to the warm pot for 1-2 minutes to dry out.
  3. Warm the butter and milk together (the microwave works). Mash and add the warm mixture gradually. Season to taste.
  4. Spread into 2 buttered oven-safe baking dishes (9x13 in), dot with butter, and cover tightly with foil.
  5. Hold hot, off the stove: put the dishes in the top oven at 200°F (about 1:30 PM). At 3:35 PM take them out and wrap them in towels in a cooler, where they stay above 140°F for about 2 hours. Just before serving, stir in a splash of warm milk to restore the creaminess. Check that they are still above 140°F.

Stuffing - Betty Crocker Bread Stuffing + golden raisins + Granny Smith apples

Chef: Anthony

Based on the Betty Crocker Bread Stuffing recipe (one batch = 9 cups bread = one 9x13 pan). Tripled for 18 people (3 pans), with raisins and apples added. Original recipe. The recipe has no stock; the butter gives the moisture.

IngredientTripled amount
Butter2 1/4 cups (4 1/2 sticks)
Celery, chopped6 large stalks
Medium onions, chopped3 (about 1 1/2 cups)
Country white bread, toasted and cubed3 loaves (1 loaf per batch, about 27 cups of cubes)
Fresh thyme leaves, chopped (or dried)1 1/2 tablespoons (or 1 1/2 tsp dried)
Salt (reduced, since the butter is salted)1 teaspoon to start for the full tripled batch (the original recipe uses 1 tsp per batch). Taste the mixture and add more if needed.
Fresh sage, finely chopped2 tablespoons (add more to taste)
Pepper3/4 teaspoon
Add-in: golden raisinsabout 1 1/2 cups
Add-in: Granny Smith apples, sliced or diced3
  1. Wednesday night: toast the country white bread, cut it into cubes, and store the cubes in bags on the counter.
  2. Thursday: chop the celery and onion (about 10:30 AM) and keep them covered on the counter.
  3. Thursday at 2:45 PM: melt the butter in a large pot (a 6-8 qt pot, or do it in two batches). Cook the celery and onion 6-8 minutes until tender. Take it off the heat.
  4. In a very large bowl or clean roasting pan, gently toss the celery mixture with the bread cubes, thyme, salt, sage, pepper, raisins, and apples until evenly coated.
  5. Divide into 3 greased 9x13 pans. Cover with foil and leave on the counter until baking at 4:15 PM (about 1 1/2 hours). A squeeze of lemon juice on the apples keeps them from browning.
  6. Bake all three pans in the bottom oven (two on one rack, one on a second rack) at 325°F, covered with foil, for 30 minutes. Then uncover and bake 15 minutes more. If the pans go in cold from the fridge, add about 10 minutes.

Anthony tosses the stuffing and slices the apples with supervision. An adult does the onion and celery chopping and the stove.

Ham with Brown Sugar Glaze

Chef: Leo

Three fully cooked Boar's Head Sweet Slice hams (about 3.25 lb each; one is apple wood smoked). Cooking instructions from the package: bake at 325°F for about 15 minutes per pound, covered tightly with foil, then uncover and bake at 425°F for the final 15 minutes with extra glaze.

  1. Take the hams out of the fridge about 30-45 minutes before heating.
  2. Glaze (make Thursday morning): stir together 1 cup brown sugar, 1/4 cup Dijon or honey, and 2-3 tablespoons water or apple juice.
  3. Put the hams in a roasting pan (all three fit in one 16x13 pan, or use two 9x13 pans) with about 1/8 inch of water in the bottom. Cover tightly with foil and bake in the top oven at 325°F for about 15 minutes per pound (about 50 minutes for 3.25 lb hams).
  4. Uncover, brush on extra glaze, raise the oven to 425°F, and bake the final 15 minutes, until the center reaches 140°F. In all, plan about 65 minutes.
  5. Rest, tented, 10-15 minutes before slicing. An adult handles the oven and carving.

Turkey Breast + Gravy

Chef: Michael

Dry brine (Wednesday evening, about 6:00 PM; 12-24 hours before roasting)

Based on the Serious Eats dry-brine method for two 6 lb bone-in breasts (12 lb total). Salt is rubbed directly on the turkey instead of soaking it in water. It seasons the meat and dries the skin, and the baking powder helps the skin brown and crisp. It needs only a half-sheet pan on one fridge shelf. You brine and roast the two fresh breasts whole (no splitting).

IngredientAmount for 2 breasts
Diamond Crystal kosher salt3 to 4 tablespoons (use 3 for lighter seasoning, 4 for fuller). If using Morton kosher, use 2 1/4 to 3 tablespoons. Do not use the Diamond Crystal amount with Morton.
Baking powder, aluminum-free (not baking soda)1 tablespoon (3 tsp)
Black pepper2 teaspoons
Orange zest (optional, for flavor)2 oranges
Finely chopped fresh thyme, sage, and rosemary (optional)1 tablespoon each of thyme and sage, 2 teaspoons rosemary
  1. Pat both breasts completely dry with paper towels. Mix the salt, baking powder, and pepper in one bowl, and divide it in half so each breast gets an equal share. Mix the orange zest and herbs in a second small bowl.
  2. Gently slide your fingers under the skin of each breast to loosen it without tearing. Rub about half of that breast's salt mixture, plus half of the zest and herbs, directly on the meat. Sprinkle the rest of the salt mixture evenly over the skin. Keep the herbs and zest under the skin, since they scorch on top at high heat.
  3. Set both breasts skin side up on a wire rack in a rimmed half-sheet pan (18x13 in), with 1-2 inches of space between them. Leave it uncovered in the fridge for 12 to 24 hours (about 19-20 hours if you start at 6:00 PM Wednesday for a 2:30 PM roast).
  4. Thursday: do not rinse and do not add more salt.

Roast (Thursday, bottom oven)

  1. About 45 minutes before roasting, take the turkey out of the fridge. Blot any wet spots with paper towels. Preheat the bottom oven to 425°F (400°F if the oven is convection).
  2. Pour about 1 cup of stock or water into the pan under the rack, so the drippings don't scorch. Brush or rub each breast with 1 to 1 1/2 tablespoons of melted unsalted butter (or vegetable oil). Keep the breasts on the rack, spaced apart so heat circulates.
  3. Roast for about 75-100 minutes (the original recipe says 65-85 for one breast, but two breasts in one oven take a bit longer), rotating the pan halfway through for even browning. Start checking at 60 minutes. Pull each breast when the thickest part (away from the bone) reaches 158°F. One may finish 5-10 minutes before the other, so pull the first one first.
  4. Move the breasts to a clean pan or cutting board and rest, tented loosely with foil, for 20-30 minutes. The temperature will rise during the rest. Check that it reaches 165°F in the thickest part before carving; if it does not, return it to the oven for a few minutes. A whole breast holds above 140°F for about an hour. For a longer hold, wrap in foil and towels or set it in a cooler.
  5. Gravy (about 6 cups): pour the pan drippings (and the stock or water under the rack) into a fat separator or measuring cup. Skim off the fat, and measure 6-8 tbsp (top up with unsalted butter if needed). Melt it in a saucepan, whisk in 6-8 tbsp all-purpose flour, and cook 2 minutes. Slowly whisk in the drippings and about 5 cups of warm stock. Simmer 8-10 minutes until it coats a spoon. Use low-sodium or unsalted stock, and taste before adding any salt, since the drippings are salty. Pepper to taste.

Green Beans

Chef: Dominic

Ingredients: 4 lb green beans, olive oil, butter, salt. Optional: lemon.

  1. Trim the beans (any time Thursday). At about 4:50 PM boil them in well-salted water for 4-5 minutes in one large pot until crisp-tender (two batches if the pot is small). Drain.
  2. Return them to the hot pot and toss with olive oil and a few tablespoons of butter.
  3. Taste before adding salt, since the butter is salted. A squeeze of lemon is optional.

Plain green beans only, with no peas or mixed vegetable blends.

Cranberry Sauce, Cornbread, Chestnuts

Chef: Dominic (cranberry sauce and chestnuts); Michael (cornbread)

  • Cranberry: chill 3 cans, then open, slice or spoon into a bowl.
  • Cornbread: bake the 2 box mixes Thursday at 9:30 AM as muffins in two muffin tins (bottom oven), following the box directions (each box: 2 eggs, 1/3 cup butter, 1/2 cup milk, so 4 eggs, 2/3 cup butter, and 1 cup milk in all). 5. Plan

Uses your double oven and 4 burners. Only the stuffing bread is done Wednesday night; everything else is cooked Thursday. The bottom oven (15 in tall) bakes the cornbread muffins in the morning, roasts the two whole turkey breasts from 2:30 PM at 425°F, then takes all three stuffing pans once the turkey is out (two on one rack, one on a second rack). The top oven (10 in tall) holds the mashed potatoes at 200°F from 1:30 PM, then heats the three small hams from 3:50 PM. Burners: no more than 3 at once, because the pasta is cooked early and the gravy is reheated in the microwave.

Why this works: nothing cooked needs fridge space. The mashed potatoes hold hot in the top oven and then a towel-wrapped cooler, the soup stays covered on the stove (about 1 1/2 hours, within the 2-hour limit), and the stuffing waits 1 1/2 hours on the counter before baking.
